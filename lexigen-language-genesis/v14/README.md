# Lexigen Language Genesis v14 — typed scene algebra

v14 is a post-failure research version derived from the visible demonstrations of the frozen v13 external campaign. Hidden v13 outputs remain sealed and were not used for development or evaluation.

## Why v14 exists

v13 selected one of fourteen human-supplied latent-generator operators. It reproduced its development families strongly, but its frozen synthesizer returned no program for all 39 valid fresh blind packages. A separate depth-two scan also showed that composing the existing v13 operators did not repair that coverage gap.

v14 replaces the flat finished-operator inventory with a typed scene algebra. Programs are short pipelines selected from demonstrations and deduplicated by semantic execution signatures.

## Scene operations

The current frozen candidate algebra contains:

- cellwise colour correspondence;
- relative movement between singleton roles;
- canvas expansion with edge projection;
- regular-grid decoding using a structural separator role;
- ordered overlay of equal partitions;
- canonical rendering of nested or occluding rectangular layers;
- completion of a unique internal blank axis;
- extension of rays from corner-marked local motifs;
- generic geometric transforms.

## Development families

One unchanged synthesizer reconstructs nine unrelated preserved failures:

1. move a singleton one step toward another singleton;
2. expand a canvas and project its boundary values;
3. infer a deterministic colour substitution;
4. decode and reflect a colour-varying regular line grid;
5. overlay four aligned partitions with learned precedence;
6. infer overlap precedence among hollow rectangular frames;
7. infer nested filled layers, including repeated colours;
8. complete a blank structural axis;
9. extend multiple direction-bearing corner motifs into rays.

The primary and portable runtimes are independently implemented. Adversarial tests cover literal-colour changes, conflict priority, repeated layers, the hollow-frame containment-cycle regression, horizontal/vertical axes and multiple simultaneous rays.

## Claim boundary

v14 is not a Language Genesis breakthrough. The generic scene atoms were designed by humans after observing visible task failures. A future v14-only sealed win would demonstrate useful external transfer and broader representation coverage, but would not prove autonomous invention of new semantics.

The next autonomous gate must construct or extend these atoms from a lower-level relational substrate without a human-authored post-failure operation.
